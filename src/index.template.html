SF Field Conditions Map

Public-data field planning. Streets are the navigation backbone; precincts are reference boundaries; operational conditions are shown where they physically occur.
Street layer: lines come from SF Public Works centerlines. “Active” means not retired, not necessarily publicly walkable. Mapped-but-not-real “paper” streets, pseudo-addressing lines, and private-parking centerlines are excluded. Each displayed segment keeps its City CNN identifier for later street-level joins. Hill colors are derived from official five-foot elevation contours and are a terrain-grade estimate, not an engineering survey.

Methodology key

How this map is computed
Street network
Downloaded at build time from SF Public Works / DataSF dataset 3psu-pn9h, “Streets – Active and Retired.” We start with active = true, then exclude source layers PAPER, PAPER_FWYS, PAPER_WATER, PSEUDO, and PRIVATE_PARKING because Public Works describes those as non-real/pseudo/parking centerlines rather than physical streets. Each displayed segment keeps its CNN, street name, endpoints, class code, source layer, and geometry.
Street styling
Public Works class codes are used only for readability: 1 freeway, 2 major street/highway, 3 arterial, 4 collector, 5 residential, 6 freeway ramp, and 0 other. “Active” only means not retired. Private streets, pedestrian-only streets, park roads, and unpaved rights-of-way can remain and are identified from the source layer field on click; the map does not claim they are publicly accessible.
Hill grade
Derived at build time from DataSF Elevation Contours rnbg-2qxw, which provides five-foot contour lines. For each street segment, the build finds contour crossings in order along the centerline. When there is enough terrain information, it reports the steepest monotonic pitch across a window of at least 80 horizontal feet; otherwise it falls back to a conservative whole-segment estimate using only the observed contour elevation range. Grade is vertical rise divided by horizontal run. This is a terrain-derived approximation, not a surveyed roadway grade, and the magnitude does not indicate uphill versus downhill direction. Freeways/ramps are not hill-emphasized for canvassing use.
Precinct boundaries
Downloaded from the SF Department of Elections / DataSF dataset d6x4-hefw, “Election Precincts — Current, Defined 2022.” Precinct polygons are reference lines only; apartment or future street conditions are not averaged into precinct scores.
What a parcel means
Here, “parcel” means a parcel-level property-lot geometry in SF Planning’s land-use dataset. It is not necessarily a building footprint: one parcel can include yards, parking, or multiple structures.
Residential-unit concentration
Downloaded from SF Planning / DataSF dataset c5ge-t6pj. We use resunits, keep only geography_type = parcel, resolve exact duplicate parcel records, and classify the remaining properties into 20–49, 50–99, 100–199, and 200+ units. If an exact duplicate has conflicting unit counts that stay within one threshold bucket, the map shows the observed range rather than false precision; a conflict crossing a threshold fails the build for review. Why this may matter for canvassing
High-unit parcels can concentrate many doors behind shared building access. The public data do not say whether a lobby is locked or whether canvassers can enter. Streets are included so these properties can be interpreted in an actual route context.
